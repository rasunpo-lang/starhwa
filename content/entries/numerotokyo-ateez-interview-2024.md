A joint interview with Hongjoong and Seonghwa published by Numéro TOKYO, tied to their April 2024 Coachella performance and the "GOLDEN HOUR: Part.1" release.

Seonghwa's Words — Translated Highlights

Seonghwa said performing at Coachella in April 2024 felt deeply meaningful — a chance to show ATINY the direction the group was heading while also proving what they'd built up to that point — and that he felt fulfilled it went well. He was also looking forward to Summer Sonic that August, a festival ATEEZ had wanted to play, and said he planned to prepare thoroughly and enjoy the stage. Asked about a moment during their second Coachella weekend when he was seen shouting mid-performance, he said it wasn't planned: singing their closing song, "WONDERLAND," brought back memories of the difficult pre-debut period and everything the group had overcome to reach that stage, and the feeling came out as that shout. Meeting ATINY afterward and hearing many of them bring it up taught him, he said, that performing with real sincerity does reach people.

On the "GOLDEN HOUR: Part.1" mini album (released May 31, 2024), he said the new chapter would show a different side of ATEEZ's music and performance than before, and that there were too many highlights to list — he'd rather people just listen for themselves. Asked about rapping on "MATZ" (from "THE WORLD EP.FIN: WILL"), he said he'd always liked rap and was glad for the chance to try it, crediting Hongjoong with shaping the track's rap style by building a guide vocal around Seonghwa's own voice, which he then adapted into his own delivery. He named Beenzino and Tyga as artists he'd been listening to a lot around that time.

On his 2024 goal of "polishing his visuals," he said his approach was simply exercising hard, since he considers "visuals" to include the body as well as the face, and that beyond that his main focus was diet management — not eating less, which he considers unhealthy, but keeping meals on a set schedule so they don't run too late. He said he was on a break from that routine at the time of the interview, planning to resume about a month ahead of the group's tour. Asked what ATEEZ means to him, he called the group his "family" — a direct translated quote — saying the members have shared so much time together, hard moments and happy ones alike, that they've become as important to him as family.
